# SoFly host outreach: the playbook

**Goal (30 days):** 15 to 20 recurring hosts in one small area, so a new visitor sees at least 10 upcoming
meetups near them. Nothing else in the plan matters if the feed is thin.

**Start here (my recommendation): Berkeley + Oakland.** You have a Berkeley email, the campus is dense with
organized groups, and one area beats five. Move on only when the numbers at the bottom say to.

## 1. Who to contact (in priority order)

Score every candidate 0 to 2 on each line and start with the highest totals:
meets **every week or two** (2), has **10 to 40 regulars** (2), has a **public organizer contact** (2),
is **inside your area** (2), is **hurt by its current tool** (1: fees, messy group chat, no sign-ups).

| # | Segment | Why they say yes | How to find them (searches to run) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **UC Berkeley student orgs and club sports** (running, climbing, pickleball, dance, language, board games, hiking) | They re-recruit every semester and already post events; officers are public. | CalLink (callink.berkeley.edu) organization directory, Cal Rec Sports club-sport pages, Berkeley subreddit and Discords. |
| 2 | **Weekly run and cycling clubs** | They meet on a fixed day, need sign-up counts, and live in group chats. | Strava: search Clubs for "Berkeley", "Oakland", "Lake Merritt". Brewery and running-store run clubs (ask the shop). |
| 3 | **Climbing and fitness gyms' community managers** | They run social nights and beginner sessions and want them full. | Climbing gyms in Berkeley and Oakland: look for "community" or "events" on their sites. |
| 4 | **Pickleball, volleyball, basketball and soccer organizers** | Court time is limited; no-shows hurt; they juggle spreadsheets. | Facebook groups and Meetup groups for "pickleball Oakland / Berkeley", city rec department league pages. |
| 5 | **Existing Meetup.com organizers** | As far as I know organizers pay Meetup a subscription; you are free and lighter. Check their current pricing before you say anything about it. | Meetup.com: search your area and pick groups with events every week and 50+ members. |
| 6 | **Board-game cafes, breweries, bookstores, libraries** | They want steady foot traffic on slow weeknights, and hosts want a place. | Google Maps: "board game cafe", "brewery" near Berkeley or Oakland; library "programs and events" pages for book clubs. |
| 7 | **Language, culture and book clubs** | Small, loyal, always looking for new members. | Library book-club listings, cultural centers, "language exchange" on Meetup and Facebook. |
| 8 | **Community connectors** (Facebook group admins, subreddit moderators, local newsletter writers) | They control attention. Ask for a partnership, never a mass post. | Message the moderators; do not post promotions in their groups. |
| 9 | **Alumni and coworking communities** | Ready-made networking and social groups. | Alumni chapter pages, coworking "community events" pages. |

Rule for all of them: use only contact details the organizer **published** for that purpose (a club's officer
page, a public contact form or email, a business's events email). Never buy or scrape lists.

## 2. The pipeline (one row per group in the tracker)

`Found → Qualified → Emailed → Replied → Call booked → Onboarded → First meetup live → Repeating host`

1. **Found and qualified.** Add to `tracker.csv` with a score. Cap yourself to the best 25 per week.
2. **Emailed.** Email 1, then follow-ups on day 4 and day 10 (templates below). After the third, stop. Send 20 to
   30 a day at most, one at a time, each personalized in the first line.
3. **Replied.** Answer within a few hours. Offer a 15-minute call or a short Loom video, their choice.
4. **Onboarded ("white glove").** Get their account created, then sit with them (or screen-share) while they
   post **their real next 4 to 8 dates as a weekly series**. Do not send them off to figure it out.
5. **First meetup live.** Attend the first one yourself and bring a few friends. Ask three questions afterwards:
   what confused you, what did you almost give up on, what would make you post the next one.
6. **Repeating host.** When they post again, ask for one introduction to another organizer. This is your
   cheapest channel.

## 3. Email templates

Write from a real address you own and sign with your real name. Keep it under 120 words. Replace anything in
[brackets]. Do not pretend to know them: one true, specific sentence about their group is worth more than any
formatting.

**Email 1, an organizer**

> Subject: Your [Tuesday run] on SoFly?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I've seen [the Tuesday run from Lake Merritt / your weekly climbing night], and it looks well organized.
> I'm building SoFly, a small free app for finding and joining local meetups, and I'm looking for a handful
> of groups to launch it with in [Berkeley/Oakland].
>
> If you'd like, I'll set up your next 4 dates for you (weekly, with sign-ups, a waitlist and reminders
> included). It's free for you and your regulars, and I'd want your honest feedback.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call, or want me to send a short video instead?
>
> [Name], [role], SoFly, so-fly-ai.vercel.app
> [Mailing address]. Not interested? Reply "no" and I won't email again.

**Follow-up 1 (day 4)**

> Subject: Re: Your [Tuesday run] on SoFly?
>
> Hi [Name], just floating this back up. The offer is that I'll post your next few [Tuesday runs] for you so
> you can see how sign-ups and reminders work, no commitment. If the timing's bad, tell me and I'll check
> back in a month.

**Follow-up 2 (day 10, the last one)**

> Subject: Last note from me
>
> Hi [Name], I don't want to fill your inbox, so this is my last message. If SoFly could ever save you the
> chore of counting sign-ups, I'm at [email]. Either way, I hope the [run] keeps growing.

**A venue (cafe, gym, brewery, library)**

> Subject: Hosting a weekly [board game night] at [Venue]?
>
> Hi [Name], I'm building SoFly, a free app that helps neighbors find local meetups. A few organizers of
> [game nights/run clubs] near [Venue] are looking for a regular, reliable place. Would you be open to being
> listed as a "host here" spot on quiet weeknights? It would bring you a steady group of regulars, and it costs
> you nothing. Could I stop by for five minutes this week?

**A student org officer**

> Subject: Free sign-ups and reminders for [Club]'s events
>
> Hi [Name], I'm a [Berkeley student/alum] building SoFly, a free app for local meetups, and I'm looking for a
> few campus groups to try it. I'll set up [Club]'s next few events for you, with sign-ups, a waitlist and
> automatic reminders, and you'd tell me what's clunky. Would you have 15 minutes this week?

**A moderator or newsletter writer (partnership, not promotion)**

> Subject: Local events partnership idea
>
> Hi [Name], I read [thing you actually read]. I'm building SoFly, a free local-meetups app. Rather than
> posting anything in your community, I'd like to ask what would actually help your members find things to
> do together. Could I share a demo and get your honest reaction?

## 4. Staying on the right side of the rules (not legal advice)

- Business email is still "commercial email" under CAN-SPAM. That means an accurate From name and address, a
  subject line that isn't misleading, your real postal address in the message, and an opt-out you honor
  promptly (the "reply no" line above, and actually stop).
- Only contact published organizer contacts. No purchased lists, no scraped personal addresses.
- Don't send bulk mail from Gmail: it will get the account flagged again. One at a time, 20 to 30 a day,
  personalized. When you reach hundreds, move to a proper email service on your own domain.
- Be honest about what SoFly is: new, free, and looking for feedback.

## 5. What "working" looks like (read these on the Admin overview, "Is it working?")

| Week | Emails sent | Calls | Hosts onboarded | Upcoming meetups near a new visitor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 60 | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| 2 | 120 | 10 | 8 | 12 |
| 3 | 180 | 16 | 14 | 20 |
| 4 | 220 | 20 | 18 | 30 |

Those are planning targets, not predictions. Cold outreach often gets single-digit to low-teens reply rates,
so expect to email 100 to 200 groups to onboard 15 to 20. After week 4, the decision numbers are:
- **Hosts who post again** (after 14 days) above about 50%: the tools work for hosts. Below 30%: fix hosting
  before you recruit more.
- **Meetups with a real crowd** above about 60%: there are enough guests. Below 40%: grow the audience (see
  the guest-side plan) before recruiting more hosts.
- **Guests who came back within 4 weeks**: this needs 4 weeks of data first. Treat 30%+ as healthy for a new app.

## 6. The tracker

Copy `tracker.csv` into a spreadsheet. One row per group. Update the stage as you go, and write the date of
the next follow-up in the last column so nothing gets dropped.
